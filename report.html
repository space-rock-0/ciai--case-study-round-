02 / Find and fix the mismatch

A media week can belong to two calendar months

TV arrives Monday–Sunday on a media calendar. Digital, email and sales arrive by calendar month. The faulty path assigns a whole week to its Monday’s month. The corrected path allocates all seven days first, then aggregates by calendar month.

Model and uncertainty

For each channel: adstock = current spend + decay × previous adstock; response = adstock / (half-saturation + adstock). Sales = intercept + linear trend + annual sine/cosine + three nonnegative channel effects. Twelve warm-up months initialize state; their sales are excluded from fitting.

A fixed, seed-independent set of 180 candidate transforms is searched for each model using constrained least squares. Decay bounds are 0.05–0.80; half-saturation bounds are 0.35–4 times training-period mean spend. The same candidate set is used across versions. Ground-truth parameters and effects never enter estimation.

200 paired circular residual bootstrap replicates use 3-month blocks and refit transform selection plus constrained coefficients. Residuals are centered and chronological designs remain fixed. All nonlinear transform candidates are refitted and reselected on every draw. The finite search and assumed response family still limit specification uncertainty coverage. The top 20 candidate fits provide a separate descriptive sensitivity check. 200 valid draws; 0 failed fits. Contribution denominators remain the original observed sales total.

Generator, known truth and limitations

Fixed seed 42; 60 analysis months plus 12 warm-up months. Independent channel variation, weekly TV bursts, seasonal baseline, trend and moderate noise. Source weeks span the entire monthly window. The generator and estimator deliberately share a response family, which favors recovery. Aggregation correction is exact only under the equal-daily-delivery assumption. Unmeasured confounding, real delivery patterns, commercial constraints, competitors and real-world causal uncertainty are outside this experiment.

True channel shares use noiseless generated effects divided by observed sales. The true counterfactual uses the generator response and shared baseline schedule. One primary seed does not establish universal performance. The fixed seeds 17, 83 and 101 improve mean share error in one case and worsen it in two; all results are retained in robustness.json. One naïve fit estimates over 100% TV share with offsetting controls, a warning about weak attribution identification. Correction remains necessary even when point-estimate error worsens.
